[felixd](/blog/what-if-the-os-were-a-log.html) runs its workloads on Felix streams, and its scale test is a good way to hurt a cluster. Three brokers, a thousand workloads, then it kills a broker. In one run with replica election turned on, it killed broker-3, which led 670 of the 2010 shards. The control plane moved 340 output streams off it, and none of them served again. They stayed silent after broker-3 came back, too ([#1914](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/issues/1914)). The same test with elections off also stalled during the outage, but it recovered 11.2 seconds after the broker returned.

Nothing had crashed, and no log line said "stuck". Every node involved was alive, answered pings, and was waiting for some other node to do something.

## A few terms

A durable stream is split into shards. Each shard has a leader, which takes the writes, and followers that hold copies. The brokers holding a copy are the shard's replica set.

A **generation** numbers a leadership. Each new leader gets a higher one than the last. Normally the control plane hands them out: it names a leader at a generation and records that in the shard's assignment.

A **ballot** is a copy's promise, kept on disk, to follow only one leader at a given generation. A copy never gives the same generation to two leaders, and it never takes a promise back. That is what stops two leaders from serving at one generation.

A **fence** is how a new leader takes over. It asks its replica set for ballots at its generation and serves only once a majority has given one. A copy that gave its ballot stops taking writes from the old leader.

With replica election on, followers can replace a dead leader without the control plane. A follower **stands**: it takes a ballot one generation up for itself and fences. If the standing doesn't open within a few seconds, it withdraws. Its ballot stays behind, because a ballot is never taken back. [Failover without the control plane](https://docs.getfelix.dev/deployment/failover-without-control-plane/) walks through all of this step by step.

## What we found

Each dead end had the same shape. One node waited for an answer, didn't get one, and kept waiting without telling anyone. The other nodes read that quiet as "busy, leave it alone".

### The leader that kept it to itself

The control plane named broker-1 leader of `wl.w377.out` at generation 509 ([#1919](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1919)). The fence reached a majority. Then broker-1 tried to take the generation on its own copy and found ballot 510 there, left by a standing it had started and withdrawn earlier.

That step answered `Pending`, which means "not yet, try again next pass". A pending fence is not reported to the control plane. So every two seconds broker-1 logged that the shard was not fenced yet, tried again and got the same answer. The control plane had named the shard once and heard nothing since. From where it sat, that looked like a leader still working on its fence.

`Pending` is right for a majority that hasn't answered yet. It was wrong here, because a ballot never goes back down and no retry at 509 could ever succeed. The fix answers `Refused` instead. The broker reports a refused generation, and the control plane names the same leader again above it.

A failover run across 2,100 streams found the next one ([#1933](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/issues/1933)). With broker-2 down, the control plane named broker-1 leader of one shard at generation 70, and broker-1 started serving. Eighty-five milliseconds later, broker-1 stood for election at 71 against broker-2.

A broker that is named leader takes its ballot before its routes are published. A candidate round on broker-1 was already running against broker-2, and it read routes that still named broker-2 at 69. It saw its copy's ballot at 70, stood one past it at 71, and withdrew three seconds later.

Now broker-1's own copy held ballot 71 while broker-1 led generation 70. A leader counts its own copy toward a majority only while that copy is at the leader's generation. It stopped counting itself, and once broker-2 came back, no commit mark was ever recorded at 70. The shard stayed open and took publishes, but it could not acknowledge any of them. All 29 of its quorum waits timed out while broker-0 confirmed every batch. Each of those publishes also held a shared client stream for the full timeout, about five seconds, so shards on the same stream missed their acknowledgements too.

The model had this right. In Felix's [TLA+](https://github.com/tlaplus/tlaplus) spec, an open leader can never stand. The code had drifted from it, and [#1936](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1936) brings it back in two ways.

First, a round no longer stands when its copy's ballot is past the route and names this broker, unless a standing on this broker wrote it. That ballot means the control plane just named us. The hold lasts at most 30 seconds, because after a restart a ballot left by a forgotten standing looks the same. A round also re-reads the routes just before it writes a ballot and gives up if they moved.

Second, a leader whose own copy holds a newer ballot of its own, with no standing under way, reports its generation refused once that has lasted 3 seconds. The delay is there because a normal promotion takes its ballot before its route lands. Then the control plane names it one generation up, as for any refusal.

### The control plane that hung instead of dying

Our cluster test for elections crashed the control plane. A crashed process refuses connections, so every request to it fails at once. A paused one accepts the connection and never answers. With the control plane paused, the scale run's elections used 3 to 12 generations each, and when the control plane resumed it marked live brokers down ([#1928](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1928)). Three waits on silence were behind it.

Each pass of a broker's routing feed waited for a fresh node catalog from the control plane, for up to 5 seconds. A standing gets 3 seconds to open, and a broker fences a shard only once a published route names it. So most standings gave up before their route went out, and the next one went a generation higher. The catalog is now read by its own task, and the feed publishes routes with the newest catalog it has. A standing's 3 seconds also count from when its route is published, not from its ballot ([#1925](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1925)).

A broker that restarts while the control plane is down can't serve until the control plane hands it its streams. It still answered pings, so its followers stopped suspecting it and never stood. A durable broker now answers a ping with `Unavailable` until it is seeded, and a pinger treats anything but a `Pong` as silence. A reply proves a process is there. It doesn't prove the process can lead.

On resume, the control plane's expiry sweep saw minutes without a heartbeat from every broker. The heartbeats had been sent. They were queued behind the pause. The control plane now notices when its own sweep stalled, meaning a gap longer than twice its interval, and doesn't count that stretch as silence from the brokers.

### The record that matched on the name

One more from the model, a cousin of the others ([#1929](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/issues/1929)). When the control plane returns, it adopts the leader the replicas elected. It compared leaders: if the one it had recorded was the one elected, it took the new generation and kept the rest of its record. But the control plane could have named c on the set {b, c, d}, been refused, and then seen c elected on its older set {a, b, c}. The record would say c leads on {b, c, d}. A later failover could promote from {b, c, d} without what a majority of {a, b, c} held.

A matching name is not a matching record. The control plane now compares the set as well. When they differ, it names c one generation up, moving from the set c was elected on to the one it recorded ([#1930](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1930)).

## When nobody reports

Fixing each dead end matters, but we won't find all of them by reading logs. So there are two backstops for a shard stuck on a leader that is alive. Both had to decide when silence is allowed to count.

The control plane's backstop ([#1923](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1923)) watches `Quorum` shards whose leader's broker is live. If the leader shows no progress at its generation for `FELIX_SHARD_STUCK_LEADER_MS` (70 seconds by default), the control plane names it again: same leader, same set, next generation. The definition of progress carries most of the weight. A first report at the generation counts. So does a report with nothing waiting on a majority, which is what an idle shard sends. So does the majority's position moving, which is what a slow shard shows. A quiet shard is not stuck. A shard with writes waiting and nothing moving is.

This is the one place the control plane acts on the absence of something, and it is safe because of what it does. It starts a new fenced term under the leader it already named. No leadership moves and no copy is declared lost. If it guessed wrong, the cost is one fence. It also holds off during a move, a fence, a set change or an election, without a live majority, and when more than 64 leaders look stuck at once, which looks more like a fault across the whole fleet than a stuck shard.

The follower's backstop ([#1925](https://github.com/GetFelix/felix/pull/1925)) covers the time the control plane is down. A follower may stand for one shard even though the leader's broker answers pings, if its copy holds a generation that never started there and nothing has arrived from the leader, no batch and no fence, for `FELIX_SHARD_SUSPECT_AFTER_MS` (120 seconds by default). The started-generation check protects idle shards. On an idle shard nothing ships, so no follower has heard from the leader in a while. But the generation started on every copy that took it, so silence alone never makes a follower stand against a healthy idle leader. Standing still needs a majority to agree in a pre-vote, and still gets one ballot per generation.

The bounds are ordered. A refusal is reported at once. A leader's stray ballot of its own is reported after 3 seconds. The control plane renames at 70 and a follower stands at 120. A rename restarts the follower's clock, so while the control plane is up it acts first, and the two never spend a generation on the same stall.

## What it costs

A rename or a standing spends a generation and a fence, and the shard pauses its writes while the fence runs. Renames of one shard back off, doubling the wait each time within an hour, and stop at four an hour. One pass renames at most sixteen.

The bounds are long on purpose. Under load a fence or a catch-up can take many seconds, and giving one up costs a generation. A stuck shard waiting a minute or two is better than a slow healthy one losing its leader.

None of this touches the publish path. At 2010 shards, the control plane's watch costs 0.52 ms per reconcile pass, against 6.3 ms for the plan the pass already makes, and passes run every 5 seconds. With three replicas per shard, an A/B run of the follower changes against the code before them, 12 rounds a side, showed no difference beyond noise: 5302 against 5264 messages a second steady, with p99 at 6.89 against 6.97 ms.

## What we learned

[The follower that forgot](/blog/the-follower-that-forgot.html) was about a leader trusting a cursor nobody had refreshed. A cursor is old evidence. Silence is no evidence at all, and each of these bugs was a node reading it as "fine".

It hid in polite places: a `Pending` retried forever and never reported, a ping answered by a broker that couldn't lead, a heartbeat gap that was really the control plane's own pause. Each fix gives the wait an end a node can observe. A refusal that will never change is reported. An answer says what the broker can actually do. A clock runs only while something that should have happened hasn't.

Where we did have to act on silence, we limited what a wrong guess can cost. The control plane's rename and the follower's standing both go through the same fence as any new leader, so a wrong guess costs a generation and never a record.
