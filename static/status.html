What's next

  1. 0.6 now

    Moving shards between brokers without downtime, with each stream's leaders spread across them, and numbers for replicated streams on a three-broker cluster. Durable streams acknowledge a publish once it is written, and a new cluster turns on the fleet features its brokers support, publisher identity aside, without an operator step. Subscriber queues are held to a byte budget; QUIC receive windows and the cache key index are next.

  2. 0.7 next

    Open in the 0.7 milestone: failover that doesn't wait on the control plane's lease, subscriptions that stop at an end offset, per-key checks on commits, reads from tiered storage, and a Go client.

  3. 1.0 later

    A stability promise, backed by a long-running public workload on Felix.

Fit

When to use Felix, and when not to

Felix is young. This is where it fits today, and where it doesn't yet.

Good fit

  • Live collaboration and fanout to many clients. A publish is encoded once however many subscribers it reaches, and a slow subscriber falls behind on its own. Browsers connect through felix-gateway.
  • Streams with state beside them. Caches with keyed watches, counters and consumer groups sit on the same log as the stream, behind one client and one set of grants.
  • Multi-tenant products. Every operation is scoped to a tenant and namespace, and tokens are narrowed to the actions and resources a tenant was granted.
  • Per-stream choices. Each stream picks in memory or durable, and Leader or Quorum acknowledgement, so one cluster can carry both throwaway and must-keep data.

Not yet

  • You need the Kafka ecosystem. Felix serves Kafka producers and consumers that assign their own partitions, but has no consumer groups or transactions over the Kafka protocol, so Kafka Connect, Kafka Streams and ksqlDB don't run.
  • You need proven scale. Published numbers come from one broker and from a three-broker cluster. Larger clusters and a long-running public workload are still to come.
  • You need Go. Clients ship for Rust, Python, TypeScript and C#. Go is not started.
  • You need a 1.0 stability promise. Felix is pre-1.0, and its API, wire protocol and semantics can change between releases.
