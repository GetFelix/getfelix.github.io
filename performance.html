Performance

Throughput and latency

One Felix broker accepts 4.4 GB/s with every message acknowledged, writes 1.4 GB/s fsynced to disk, and acknowledges a single fsynced publish in 0.31 ms — about a third of a millisecond. Every number here comes from the v0.6.0 performance write-up, which has the exact conditions and how to reproduce them.

Latency

Fast when idle, predictable when saturated

With few publishes in flight, Felix acks in well under a millisecond. Push it to full throughput with thousands outstanding and acks wait in line for their turn: the wait is the work outstanding divided by the rate it completes, as Little's law predicts, and the measured p50s land on that prediction. Keep few requests in flight for sub-millisecond acks, or many for throughput. Two kinds of latency works through the numbers.

Comparison

Against NATS JetStream

NATS ran tuned, on the same VMs, with the same 64 publishers and payloads, interleaved with Felix. With both fsyncing every write before the ack, Felix is 1.8 to 3.5 times faster. With periodic fsync on both sides it is 1.25 times faster at 4 KiB and 9.5 times at 256 B, and 1.2 times in memory. Core NATS is 1.34 to 1.39 times faster at fire-and-forget, where it discards messages nobody is subscribed to.

The two systems are built differently, so no pair is an exact match. Read each ratio with where it still differs. The 14.8× durable batch pair is partly the benchmark tool: nats bench sends one atomic batch at a time, while Felix keeps 64 in flight. Full results are in Felix against NATS JetStream.
